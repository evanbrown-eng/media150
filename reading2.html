Reading 2

The message behind both media texts are clear in that generative AI and LLM’s are dangerous for our cognitive development and maintenance. There is ongoing studies and research that supports this, but even with all this knowledge and caution, AI use continues to rise, but why? The key gaps that raised more questions than answers are all centered around the reason behind public reliance on AI and realistic solutions to developing ourselves, our relationships, and our connection to the world without an overreliance on AI.
The brain on LLM study exhibits that there is a cognitive cost for writing an essay with ChatGPT, so how do we build upon that information to better ourselves? Yes, one could argue that the data alone can convince more students and educators to not use LLMs, but that can be a flimsy argument at best. We are already in a world of AI generated works and texts. Generative emails, messages, and essays that can be sent in an instant. Instead of just telling people to stop using it, a deeper analysis must be conducted.
When it comes to education, to build upon this information to better students and their development, I believe we must first ask why would students use ChatGPT to write essays in the first place? The first answer many go to is laziness. Essays are not easy for the “average” American student so they take the easy route and get someone to do it for them, but instead of paying someone to write for them in old days of 10-15 years ago, they ask ChatGPT to do it for them even though if they just buckle down, put it in elbow grease, and ask for just a bit of help, they can create not just an essay as good as ChatGPT, but a better one. I propose that it can only work in a vacuum.
When it comes to the YouTube video, “Brain Experts WARNING: Watch This Before Using ChatGPT Again!”, out of all the topics discussed in which AI can and is affecting our livelihoods, the topic on AI girlfriends stood out the most to me. Just like when it comes to education, gaps arise when trying to dissect the reason behind the rise and usage of such applications.
Discussions of the loneliness epidemic have been happening as far back as COVID, especially among males in industrialized societies and we are able to observe the effects of this epidemic through such as parasocial relationships with content creators and now romantic AI partners, but even though the cause for this is more understood and spotlighted than education, how we still struggling to enact and reinforce healthy solutions?
AI is doing more than what it’s companies are promising. It’s filling in gaps in the modern human experience. Gaps that were always there, but are now opportunistically filled by AI and as conscious consumers, users, participants, and observers, we must take a step back, and look at how we got here.
